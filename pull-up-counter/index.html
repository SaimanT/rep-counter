One pull-up at a time

Pull-ups are where “just one” matters most: for many people one rep is the whole set. Do one, drop down, tap the counter. Rest if you need to. Then one more. Save each set with Enter, and the counter adds up how many pull-ups you did in the whole workout — often more than you expected.

Controls: Space +1, Backspace −1, Enter finishes a set, R resets, the 1× button hides the goal. On a phone, tap the screen. About the one-rep method.

If even one pull-up is hard

  1. Dead hang — just hang from the bar; count seconds or hangs.
  2. Scapular pulls — from the hang, pull your shoulders down without bending your arms.
  3. Negatives — jump or step up to the top and lower yourself slowly.
  4. Assisted pull-ups — with a band or a foot on a chair.

How many pull-ups should you do?

Many short sets beat one hard one. If you can do 1–3, do 5–6 sets of a single rep with rest in between. A ladder (1, 2, 3…) works well too — save each step with Enter.

Form in brief

  • Start from a dead hang with straight arms, no swinging.
  • Chin above the bar.
  • Drive your elbows down; don’t just bend your arms.

FAQ

I can’t do a single pull-up. How do I get my first one?

Practise negatives and dead hangs 2–3 times a week. Count every negative as a rep — they build the strength for the first real pull-up.

Can I track a ladder workout?

Yes. Press Enter after each step — you’ll see every set, like “1 · 2 · 3 · 4”, and the total.

Is it free, and does it work offline?
