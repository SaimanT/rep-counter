One squat at a time

Stand facing the screen, phone in your hand or on a shelf. Squat once, stand up, tap. That’s it — one done. Now just the next one. The numbers are readable from a couple of metres away, so you never have to stop and check.

Controls: Space +1, Backspace −1, Enter finishes a set, R resets, the 1× button hides the goal. On a phone, tap the screen. About the one-rep method.

If even one squat is hard

  1. Chair squats — sit down onto a chair and stand back up.
  2. Assisted squats — hold a door frame or a table for balance.
  3. Half squats — go only as deep as feels steady.
  4. Full squats — thighs to parallel or lower.

How many squats should you do?

Start with what you can do with good form — even 5 chair squats count. Then add a couple of reps per set every week. For a “100 squats a day” challenge, use sets of 10–25 with rest in between; the counter keeps the total.

Form in brief

  • Feet shoulder-width apart, toes slightly out.
  • Knees follow your toes and don’t cave in.
  • Back straight, heels on the floor.

FAQ

Squats hurt my knees. What can I do?

Try chair squats with a shallower depth and keep your knees in line with your toes. If pain continues, check with a doctor or physiotherapist.

How many squats should I do a day?

30–50 over a few sets is a good start; trained people do 100 or more. Increase gradually.
