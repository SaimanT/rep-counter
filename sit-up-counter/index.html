One crunch at a time

Put your phone on the mat next to you. Do one crunch, tap the screen, lie back. Then only the next one. The numbers are big enough to read lying down, and you don’t have to count under your breath while your abs are burning.

Controls: Space +1, Backspace −1, Enter finishes a set, R resets, the 1× button hides the goal. On a phone, tap the screen. About the one-rep method.

If even one sit-up is hard

  1. Dead bug — on your back, slowly lower the opposite arm and leg.
  2. Partial crunches — lift only your shoulder blades off the floor.
  3. Seated knee raises — on the edge of a chair, pull your knees up.
  4. Full sit-ups or leg raises — when the above feel easy.

How many reps?

3 sets of what you can do with control, 3–4 times a week, then add a few reps per set. Save each exercise as its own set with Enter.

Form in brief

  • Lower back on the floor; don’t pull on your head.
  • Lift with your abs, slowly, without jerking.
  • Breathe out on the effort.

FAQ

Sit-ups strain my neck. What should I do?

Switch to partial crunches or dead bugs and keep your chin slightly tucked. Your hands support your head; they don’t pull it.

How many sit-ups should I do a day?

3–4 sets of 15–30 controlled reps, 3–4 times a week, is usually enough. Your abs need rest days too.
