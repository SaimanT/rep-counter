One lunge at a time

Step, lower, come back up, tap. One done. With alternating lunges every step counts, so a goal of 20 means 10 per leg. If you do one leg first and then the other, save them as separate sets with Enter — and still think only about the next step.

Controls: Space +1, Backspace −1, Enter finishes a set, R resets, the 1× button hides the goal. On a phone, tap the screen. About the one-rep method.

If even one lunge is hard

  1. Supported split squat — hold a chair or a wall, feet staggered, move only up and down.
  2. Short reverse lunge — a small step back, shallow depth.
  3. Full reverse lunge — both knees close to 90°.
  4. Forward or walking lunges — the hardest version.

How many lunges should you do?

Start with 2–3 sets of 5–8 per leg and add two reps a week. Balance first, numbers second.

Form in brief

  • A step long enough for both knees to bend to about 90°.
  • Front knee over the foot, not caving in.
  • Torso upright; lower straight down.

FAQ

Do I count lunges per leg or both together?

Whichever you prefer — just count the same way every workout. Most people save each leg as its own set with Enter.

I lose my balance in lunges. What helps?

Hold a chair or a wall and start with split squats, where your feet don’t move. Balance improves quickly with practice.

Is it free, and does it work offline?

Yes: no sign-up, no ads, and after the first visit the page works without internet. Your count is saved in the browser.
