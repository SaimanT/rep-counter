One push-up at a time

Put your phone on the floor in front of your hands, or your laptop where you can see it from the plank. Do one push-up and tap the screen (or press space). Then think only about the next one. The counter holds the number, so your head is free for the push-up itself.

Controls: Space +1, Backspace −1, Enter finishes a set, R resets, the 1× button hides the goal. On a phone, tap the screen. About the one-rep method.

If even one push-up is hard

Make the push-up easier instead of skipping it. Each step up is real progress:

  1. Wall push-ups — hands on a wall, body straight.
  2. Incline push-ups — hands on a table, then a bench, then a low step.
  3. Knee push-ups — on the floor, from your knees.
  4. Full push-ups — even one is a win.

How many push-ups should you do?

Start with what you can do today — five clean reps are worth more than twenty sloppy ones. A common path: 3 sets of what you can, then add one rep per set every few workouts. For the “100 push-ups a day” challenge, spread the hundred over the day in small sets — the counter adds them up.

Form in brief

  • A straight line from head to heels; don’t let your hips sag.
  • Elbows at about 45° to your body, not flared out.
  • Chest close to the floor, then arms fully straight.

FAQ

I can’t do a single push-up. Where do I start?

Start against a wall. Do one, tap, then one more. When ten wall push-ups feel easy, move your hands lower — a table, then a bench, then your knees.

How many push-ups should I do a day?

Beginners do well with 10–30 spread over a few sets, trained people with 50–100. Consistency matters more than the number.
