When even one rep is hard

Some days the hard part isn’t counting. It’s that every single rep takes everything you have. You gather all your willpower for one push-up — and then your head says “and nineteen more”. That thought is heavier than the rep itself.

This counter is built for those days. You don’t have to hold the number in your head or look at how far the goal is. Do one rep. Press. The counter remembers it. Now there is only the next one.

The one-rep method

  1. Gather yourself for one rep — not for ten. Just this one.
  2. Do it and press. That rep is done and counted. It can’t be taken away.
  3. Start again from “just one”. Not “eight to go” — one. The counter keeps the rest.

Ten reps done this way aren’t ten. They are one, ten times.

Why it helps

  • One rep feels possible. A big number feels like a wall; a single rep is a step.
  • Every press closes a loop. You get a small finished thing, again and again — that’s what progress feels like.
  • Nothing to keep in your head. All your attention goes to the rep, not to arithmetic.
  • You choose whether to see the goal. On good days the “left” line pulls you to the finish. On hard days turn on “Just one” mode and see only what you’ve already done.

How to use the counter

  • Set a goal if you like — or don’t look at it at all (see “Just one” mode below).
  • Do one rep, then press space. On a phone, tap anywhere on the screen.
  • Miscounted? Backspace removes one rep.
  • Finished a set? Press Enter. The set is saved and you start again from one. Your sets add up below: “Sets: 5 · 4 · 4 — workout total: 13”.
  • “Just one” mode (the 1× button at the top) hides the goal, the countdown and the progress bar. Only the number of reps you’ve done stays on screen.
  • On a phone the same actions are buttons under the counter, and the screen stays on while the page is open.

FAQ

What if I can’t do even one rep?

Make the rep easier, not the number bigger: push-ups against a wall, squats onto a chair, a dead hang instead of a pull-up. One easy rep beats zero, and each exercise page lists easier versions.

Should I set a goal?

Yes, but you don’t have to look at it. Turn on “Just one” mode and the counter keeps the goal for you, showing only how many reps you’ve done.

Can I use it on my phone?

Yes. Every tap on the screen adds one rep, and the screen stays on while the page is open.

Is it free, and does it work offline?

Yes: no sign-up, no ads, and after the first visit the page works without internet. Your count is saved in the browser.
